AI Research

Theory of Mind for Multi-Agent Strategic Reasoning

Opponent modeling, memory, self-play, GRPO and PPO.

Motivation

Multi-agent LLM systems (AutoGen, OWL, GAIA-style assistants) mostly target collaboration or assistance settings where all agents share a goal. Real-world agents, however, often act in non-cooperative or negotiation settings that require strategic reasoning. Meanwhile, LLM agents are trained with single-agent RL paradigms (PPO, DPO, GRPO): the agent generates token trajectories, gets a reward, and is optimized — with no interaction with other agents during training. At test time, deployment is inherently multi-agent: every agent's action affects the others and the environment is shaped by that interaction. This training/test mismatch means pretraining approximates only some forms of human strategic reasoning, and this fails to transfer to genuine multi-agent interaction — even with chain-of-thought prompting.

Research goal

Introduce multi-agent RL primitives into single-agent RL so agents trained in isolation can act effectively in society — while keeping standard single-agent techniques (PPO, DPO, GRPO) rather than building a full multi-agent RL stack from scratch. The target behavior: get the best payoff in cooperative settings, competitive settings, repeated games, and negotiation.

Datasets and models

Training uses simple matrix games with dense rewards, full information, and few agents (Battle of the Sexes, First-Price Sealed-Bid Auction, Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma, Negotiation Games). Testing uses complex, sparser-reward, partial-information language environments (Avalon, Rock-Paper-Scissors, Repeated Matching Pennies, the Ultimatum Game) to evaluate generalization of the learned strategic reasoning.

Model families (DeepSeek and Qwen, 7B scale) were compared across three reasoning conditions — no reasoning (base instruct model), prompt-based reasoning (SCoT), and learned reasoning (DeepSeek-R1 / QwQ-32B) — to isolate the effect of reasoning from model scale and family.

Pipeline

  1. Outcome-based pairing: generate multiple trajectories, compare final payoffs — higher payoff is chosen, lower is rejected.
  2. Equilibrium-guided labeling: label trajectories by game-theoretic reasoning (e.g., Nash equilibrium bid shading in the auction), not just lucky outcomes.
  3. Self-play with ELO filtering: rank strategies by ELO to automatically separate strong from weak play.
  4. Opponent-conditioned contrastive pairs: same state, different reasoning — opponent-aware reasoning is chosen over greedy/naive reasoning.
  5. DPO fine-tunes on these preference pairs; GRPO with multi-agent rollouts then samples candidate actions, simulates opponent responses, computes rewards, and updates toward the best action.
  6. Opponent modeling levels for evaluation: no modeling (baseline) → fixed opponent assumption → adaptive modeling → recursive Theory-of-Mind reasoning.
  7. Inference-time strategies: prompt-based opponent reasoning (opponent history in the prompt) or explicit opponent simulation (predict the opponent's next move, then choose an action).

Example DPO pair (opponent-aware reasoning, Battle of the Sexes)

After two rounds where the opponent consistently chose Football while the model chose Opera (payoff 0 both times), the chosen response reasons that the opponent's revealed preference makes coordination more valuable than the model's own preference, and switches to Football (expected reward 2/round). The rejected response greedily picks Opera because it has the highest nominal payoff (3), ignoring the opponent's revealed behavior — and earns 0.

Results

Multi-agent GRPO rollouts improved strategic decision-making on a 4×4 Tic-Tac-Toe testbed against TinyLlama: reward for the first-move player rose over training while approx. regret fell early, though with some later fluctuation. Radar-chart analysis of strategic personality (HEXACO-style traits inferred from behavior against Always-Cooperate / Always-Defect / Tit-for-Tat / Alternating opponents) showed a clear progression: DeepSeek Base was cooperative but easily exploited; DeepSeek + SCoT was more opponent-aware but still cooperation-leaning; Qwen Base was fair but rigid; Qwen + SCoT was the most balanced, adaptive, and strategically flexible of the four.

Evaluation metrics

Reward (payoff improvement across multi-agent interactions) and regret (gap between the chosen action and the optimal strategy), estimated via GRPO rollout evaluation: sample candidate actions, simulate opponent responses, compute reward & regret, update toward the best action.

Related work grounding this project

Motivated by findings that multi-agent collaboration frameworks (AutoGen, OWL) outperform single-agent approaches but mostly target cooperative settings; that LLM bargaining and negotiation benchmarks show strategic reasoning is genuinely hard for LLMs (e.g., OG-Narrator raising deal rate from 26.67% to 88.88% in buyer-seller bargaining); that PPO/DPO/GRPO are the standard single-agent RL toolchain; and that behavioral game-theory evaluations show model scale and CoT prompting do not reliably improve strategic reasoning on their own — motivating explicit opponent-aware training rather than relying on scale alone.
